## What it measures

The Health System Index combines three pillars:

- **Outcomes**: life expectancy, the under-5 mortality rate and the maternal mortality ratio.
- **Access to care**: physicians and hospital beds per person, measles immunisation coverage, and access to basic sanitation.
- **Financial protection**: health spending per person, and the share of health spending paid out of pocket by households (lower is better).

## What it does not measure

- It does not measure the quality of care, waiting times or patient satisfaction, which have no comparable open data for all countries.
- It does not measure mental health, or non-communicable diseases beyond their effect on life expectancy.
- Outcomes reflect much more than the health system: income, diet, conflict and environment all matter.

## Why these weights

Outcomes get 40% because they are what health systems are ultimately for. Access gets 35%. Financial protection gets 25%, because people who cannot afford care go without it, even when it exists. Health spending per person is log-scaled, so the step from $50 to $100 counts as much as the step from $5,000 to $10,000.

## Known limitations

- **Modelled estimates.** Maternal mortality and several mortality figures are modelled estimates (UN Inter-agency groups), marked EST on the site.
- **Inputs are not efficiency.** More physicians and beds count as better, though a health system can achieve good outcomes with fewer.
- **Missing data.** Countries are ranked only if they have data for at least 70% of the weight and at least 500,000 people.
