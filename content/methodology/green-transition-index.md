## What it measures

The Green Transition Index tracks progress towards a low-carbon, low-pollution economy:

- **Clean energy**: renewable energy's share of final energy use, renewable electricity's share of power output, and access to clean cooking fuels.
- **Emissions & resource use**: greenhouse gas emissions per person, energy used per unit of GDP, and material footprint per person. Lower is better for all three.
- **Air & nature**: population-weighted exposure to fine-particle air pollution (lower is better), and the share of land under protection.

## What it does not measure

- It does not measure historical responsibility for emissions, or emissions embodied in imports (except through the material footprint).
- It does not judge climate policy or targets. It measures outcomes.
- It does not measure biodiversity directly. Protected area is a proxy, and protection on paper is not always protection in practice.

## A note on poorer countries

Low-income countries often score well on emissions because they consume little, and badly on clean cooking. A high renewable share can also reflect traditional biomass, such as wood fuel, rather than modern clean energy. Read the pillar scores, not just the total.

## Why these weights

Clean energy and emissions get 40% each. They are the core of decarbonisation. Air quality and protected nature get 20%. Within emissions, total greenhouse gases per person count most (50%).

## Known limitations

- **Different years.** Renewable energy shares lag by two to three years.
- **Water and air estimates.** PM2.5 exposure is a modelled estimate.
- **Missing data.** Countries are ranked only if they have data for at least 70% of the weight and at least 500,000 people.
