## What it measures

The Digital Readiness Index estimates how prepared a country is to take part in a digital economy, across three pillars:

- **Access**: how many people use the internet, how many have fixed broadband, and how widespread mobile subscriptions are.
- **Skills**: tertiary enrolment and adults' average years of schooling, as broad proxies for the ability to use digital tools.
- **Digital economy**: how much of the country's service exports are telecom, computer and information services, how much of its manufactured exports are high-technology, and the number of secure internet servers per person.

## What it does not measure

- It does not measure connection speed, price or quality. Those data are mostly proprietary.
- It does not measure digital skills directly. Enrolment and schooling are proxies.
- It says nothing about online freedom, privacy or cybersecurity.

## Why these weights

Access gets the largest weight (40%) because it is the precondition for everything else. Skills and the digital economy get 30% each. Within Access, internet use counts most (40%) because it measures actual use rather than subscriptions. Secure servers are log-scaled because a few countries host very large numbers.

## Known limitations

- **Small exporters.** ICT and high-tech export shares can swing with a few large firms or re-exports.
- **Mobile subscriptions** can exceed 100 per 100 people, because one person may hold several SIMs.
- **Missing data.** Countries are ranked only if they have data for at least 70% of the weight, and only countries with at least 500,000 people are eligible.
