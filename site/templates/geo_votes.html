Geopolitics · UN General Assembly votes

UN alignment

Every year the UN General Assembly holds dozens of recorded votes, on everything from Ukraine and Gaza to nuclear weapons and human rights. How often two countries vote the same way is one of the most-used measures of alignment in international relations research.

Reading the map

Each country gets an “ideal point” estimated from all its General Assembly votes (Bailey, Strezhnev and Voeten 2017). Higher values mean voting closer to the United States and Western democracies; lower values, closer to the developing-world majority. Unlike raw agreement, ideal points are comparable across years.
