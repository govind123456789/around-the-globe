How the live estimates work

The ticking numbers are simple calculations from published annual figures. Nobody is counting births, deaths or emissions in real time.

The calculation

  • Population now = the latest mid-year population, grown continuously at (birth rate − death rate) from 1 July of that year.
  • Births and deaths this year = annual rate × population now × the share of the calendar year that has passed (UTC).
  • Net growth this year = births − deaths.
  • CO₂ this year = CO₂ per person × population now × the share of the year that has passed. This assumes emissions stay at their latest known level per person, and excludes land-use change.

What this means

  • These are estimates, not measurements. Precision to the last digit is only an effect of the arithmetic.
  • They assume recent rates simply continue, and don’t capture shocks such as a pandemic or a war.
  • Year-to-date counts reset each 1 January and assume events are spread evenly through the year.
  • For anything you plan to cite, use the published annual figures, not the live numbers.
